Take-home assignment: Technical Product Manager —
Claims + Ledger Module
Context
We’re building a modern, cloud-native, multi-tenant dental PMS. This assignment focuses on
one of the most complex and high-impact areas: insurance claims and the financial ledger.

We’re looking for strong product judgment, system thinking, and technical depth. There’s no
single “correct” solution—what matters is that your solution is coherent, implementable, and
defensible, and that you can explain tradeoffs clearly.




Problem to solve
Design v1 of a Claims + Ledger module for a dental PMS.

The module should support:

     ●​ Creating insurance claims from procedures/treatment data
     ●​ Submitting claims (direct payer or clearinghouse-style integration) (consider multiple
        clearinghouse, patient self pay)
     ●​ Tracking claim statuses and payer responses
     ●​ Receiving remittances (ERA/EOB) (dropboxes)
     ●​ Posting payments, adjustments, write-offs, denials, reversals
     ●​ Maintaining an accurate patient and insurance ledger with strong auditability
        (patient, guarantor relationship and ledger)
     ●​ Producing reliable balance views and reconciliation hooks

Assume:

     ●​ The PMS is multi-tenant (many practices; potentially multi-location orgs) (consider
        multi-state too)
     ●​ We operate in the US dental billing context
     ●​ High correctness + auditability is required
     ●​ Integrations may be unreliable (retries, duplicates, delays)

5) Reliability, idempotency, and reconciliation
This section is critical. Cover:
How you handle duplicate inputs:

   ●​ Same ERA imported twice
         ○​ If two or more 8335s have the same claim number, date, and payment amount,
            then that charge may be a duplicate
                 ■​ In this case, skip ingestion and log as a duplicate
         ○​ Check each CLP
   ●​ Duplicate clearinghouse callbacks
         ○​ This can happen due to duplicate submission, system errors or incorrect routing
                 ■​ Easiest approach is to establish a process in claims response service to
                     enable unique claim update sequence number by checking claim#, NPI,
                     date of service, claims lines
                 ■​ Automated claims scrubber to detect and dedupe before claims response
                     service
                 ■​ Setting up status
                 ■​ Overall hash-based deduping, event-level idempotency for callbacks and
                     idemptency keys for all submission and status operation.
     ●​ Concurrency:
             ○​ Two users are posting at the same time
             ○​ partial failure while posting a multi-line remittance
                 ■​ When 2 users attempts to post at the same time, the first transaction to
                     commi\t increments the version.and the 2nd detects the mismatch. The
                     design here could be that every acting include an idempotency key like:
                     ERA_id+Claim_ID+Line_number
                 ■​ The design should also prevent ledger from being updated twice
                 ■​ For partial failure, when posting a multi-line remittance - for example if 3
                     out of 5 lines were updated and 2 did not update correctly, the system
                     should be allowed to committed linked as successful. However, the lines
                     that were not updated must be flagged. There should be systemmatic
                     approach for reprocessing the remittance and applying the flagged lines
                     using the idempotency keys. This guarantees no duplicate and avoids
                     rolling back the entire remittance. This is a better approach especially
                     when there are claims with large number of lines.
     ●​ Reconciliation:
             ○​ how you detect “drift” between stored balances and ledger truth
             ○​ what monitoring/alerts you’d put in place
             ○​ basic strategy for repair (backfill/recompute/replay)
                 ■​ Ledger is the source of truth
                 ■​ There should be a periodic job that recomputes balances from the ledger
                     and compares with stored aggregates. Any intolerance must be logged
                     as drift (preferabbly with ID, delta and last change timestamp)
                 ■​ For monitoring there should be drift metrics, error budgets, event logs and
                     audit trails
                  ■​ For repair strategy, recompute is done when the drift is detected and
                     recompute id done directly from immutable ledger.
                  ■​ If the drift is from partial failure similar to the example above, replay the
                     event using the idempotency keys. This regenerates the correct entries
                  ■​ Backfill is done due to incorrect data or a logic bug. Here, compensating
                     entries are made to the ledger, preserving the audit trail.

●​ Data migration approach (if migrating from another PMS vs greenfield)
    ○​ Migration from another PMS:
          ■​ There is a common assumption that claims systems are standardized
              across the industry and that data migration is a standard set of rules.
              Unfortunately, that isn’t true. Every clinic, EHR system, and payor system
              stores and structures data differently, and each organization often has its
              own unique operational and reporting needs.
          ■​ Key edge case included in V1: In some scenarios, it is necessary to
              retain historically closed claims. This is critical for duplicate-claim
              validation, frequency limits, active legal or audit inquiries, and claims
              associated with accidents, particularly those involving minors.
          ■​ Key edge cases you intentionally defer: Even if there is no legal or
              compliance requirement to do so, it is still recommended to obtain a full
              export of the data warehouse. A full migration isn’t necessary, but having
              the datadump provides valuable historical reference. Since the clinic can
              still meet its legal and audit obligations with the prior billing vendor for 6-7
              years after the migration.
          ■​ The most effective approach is to surface these gaps early in the product
              roadmap. Identifying data-model differences, workflow variations, and
              integration constraints up front to plan the right migration or export
              strategy.
          ■​ Note: Migration has historically been the critical path, most complex, and
              high-risk component to RCM change.
          ■​ This approach requires resource bandwidth
    ○​ Greenfield:
          ■​ This approach significantly reduces the implementation complexity by
              avoiding early heavy_lift migration
          ■​ Streamlines onboarding for new clinics
          ■​ Good for clinics that prefer to complete migration only after the RCM
              engine is stable and the core workflow is validated in production
          ■​ Staff may end up working in 2 systems until the old AR runs off.
